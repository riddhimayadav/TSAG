Designing the Dashboard Fans Actually Feel

We mapped every screen inside Moody Center—from the technical director's LED board to the volunteer-run merch iPads. That audit fed into a stacked KPI tree that now powers the new dashboard.

Layer 1 Arrival pulse (gates open pace, scan-to-seat times, drop-off congestion)
Layer 2 Atmosphere dial (decibel, crowd heatmaps, chant triggers)
Layer 3 Spend runway (per-cap, SKU velocity, POS downtime alerts)

What We Learned From Dell

The Dell smart stadium team joined us for a fireside. Their advice:

  • Pair any new sensor deployment with a “paper prototype.” If staff can’t sketch what the data should look like, you aren’t ready to automate it.
  • Use “fan SWAT” crews. Dell rotates analysts into stands to annotate what the charts miss.
  • Design for contingency. Scoreboard outages, Wi-Fi slowdowns, and power anomalies need playbooks stored in the same dashboard.

Rolling the Playbook Into BOSSO Projects

We distilled the engagement into three standards for every BOSSO ops sprint:

  1. Every pod presents a one-page “narrative deck” before showing charts.
  2. Live dashboards must include a “what to do now” panel written in plain language.
  3. Analytics pods and production pods pair program their deliverables twice per semester.
